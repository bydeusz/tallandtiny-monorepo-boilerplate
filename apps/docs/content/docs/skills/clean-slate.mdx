`clean-slate` tidies your dev environment: it shows you every git **worktree** with an honest
status, lets you pick which to remove, and clears out the **superpowers** folders — without
ever silently destroying work or the session you're running in.

<Callout type="info">
  **Last updated** 2026-07-16 · **Applies to** `.claude/skills/project/skills/clean-slate/`
</Callout>

## What it does

Building tickets leaves clutter behind: isolated **worktrees** (separate working copies of
the repo, one per branch) pile up, and the superpowers workflow leaves specs, plans and
scratch files. `clean-slate` clears both. It inspects every worktree, presents them as a
**numbered list with a plain status on each**, and lets _you_ choose which to remove —
including ones that are in use. What it guarantees: nothing is removed unless you selected it;
any worktree that carries a warning (uncommitted work, unpushed commits, or a live session) is
called out and only force-removed after you confirm that specific one; and the worktree this
session is running in is never removed.

A couple of terms:

- A **worktree** is a second checkout of the repo on its own branch, so work stays isolated;
  [start-ticket](/docs/skills/start-ticket) creates these.
- The **superpowers folders** are `docs/superpowers/` (design specs + plans, git-tracked) and
  `.superpowers/` (local scratch and logs, gitignored).

## When to use it

Reach for it when your **workspace has piled up and you want a fresh start**:

- Old worktrees from finished (merged) tickets are cluttering the repo.
- Superpowers specs, plans and scratch from past work are lying around.
- You want to reset your environment deliberately — and decide item by item what goes.

**When _not_ to use it:**

- **You just want to drop one specific branch or file.** This is a guided sweep of worktrees
  and the superpowers folders, not a general-purpose delete tool.
- **From the worktree you want gone.** It can't remove the session's own worktree — run it
  from another session to remove that one.

## How to start it

Ask for it by name — **`/project:clean-slate`** — or in plain language:

- "ruim mijn worktrees op" · "gooi worktrees weg" · "opschonen" · "reset mijn omgeving" · "verwijder superpowers"
- "clean slate" · "clean up my worktrees"

## What happens

1. **List the worktrees.** It works from the main checkout (protecting the current session's
   worktree) and inspects each worktree, folding it down to one honest status: **clean**,
   **uncommitted work**, **unpushed commits**, **live session (locked)**, or **current session
   (protected)**. Rebuild noise like `graphify-out/` is filtered out so clean worktrees don't
   look scary.
2. **You pick.** It shows the numbered list and you reply with the numbers to remove — a range,
   `all`, or `none`.
3. **Confirm the risky ones.** For each worktree you picked that carries a warning, it restates
   the specific risk and gets a final go before force-removing that one — never a blanket force.
4. **Remove.** It removes the selected worktrees, then clears the superpowers folders — the
   tracked `docs/superpowers/` with a commit, the gitignored `.superpowers/` scratch by deleting
   it.
5. **Report.** It confirms what was removed, what was kept and why, and that git's internal
   bookkeeping matches.
